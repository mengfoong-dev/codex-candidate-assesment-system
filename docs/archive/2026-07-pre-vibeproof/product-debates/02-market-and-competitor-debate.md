# Market and Competitor Debate

## Market conclusion

Malaysia has credible demand for better AI-readiness assessment, but this does not automatically prove demand for a new standalone coding platform.

Jobstreet reported in 2025 that approximately 70% of Malaysian employers considered or assessed AI capability during hiring, while only 26% of recruiters were using AI hiring tools. It also reported hesitation related to reduced personalisation. This suggests interest alongside an adoption and trust gap.

Source: [Jobstreet Hiring, Compensation and Benefits Report 2025](https://my.jobstreet.com/about/news/article/hiring-compensation-benefits-report-2025)

TalentCorp's workforce impact study identified major AI- and digital-driven changes across ICT, Global Business Services, electrical and electronics, and other Malaysian sectors.

Source: [TalentCorp Impact Study](https://www.talentcorp.com.my/impact-study/)

The market hypothesis is therefore:

> Some Malaysian hiring teams need a short and understandable way to verify AI-assisted engineering ownership without adopting a large enterprise assessment programme.

This hypothesis still requires customer interviews and pilot evidence.

## Claims VibeProof must not make

- `We are the first platform to allow AI during coding assessments.`
- `No competitor tracks candidate process.`
- `No competitor uses realistic engineering work.`
- `Our scenario generator is unique.`
- `We can detect whether a candidate used AI.`
- `We know what a candidate is thinking.`

## Why employers may not already use an alternative

These are hypotheses to validate, not established facts:

1. The company hires too few engineers to justify a full assessment platform.
2. Existing interviews and take-home exercises feel familiar enough.
3. Creating and calibrating job-relevant tasks requires technical time.
4. Long assessments create candidate drop-off.
5. Recruiters may not understand technical scores without supporting evidence.
6. Procurement, integration, privacy, and governance create adoption effort.
7. The employer may need verification of a specific portfolio or take-home submission rather than another standardised test.

## Recommended initial segment

The strongest initial segment to validate is Malaysian junior, graduate, and internship engineering hiring where:

- Application volume is meaningful.
- Portfolios or take-home work are common.
- Technical interviewer time is constrained.
- Employers want candidates who can work responsibly with AI.
- A 10- to 15-minute verification step is acceptable.

Large enterprises may already use established platforms. Very small businesses may not hire often enough. Recruitment agencies, graduate programmes, startups, Global Business Services teams, and mid-sized technical employers may provide a more realistic entry point.

## Regulatory design requirement

Malaysia's Automated Decision-Making and Profiling Guideline uses algorithmic candidate ranking and shortlisting as an employment example with potentially significant effects. VibeProof should therefore provide notice, transparent evidence, consent controls, and human review rather than automatic rejection.

Source: [Malaysia Automated Decision-Making and Profiling Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Automated-Decision-Making-And-Profiling-Guideline-ADMP.pdf)
